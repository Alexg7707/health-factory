How to start a conversation about sex

“Can we talk about last night?” can leave a partner guessing. A few more words can tell them what’s on your mind and give them room to say what’s on theirs.

Find a moment to talk

Suppose you’re having coffee together and you’d like to bring up last night. Start with the part you already understand about yourself. “I really liked it when you asked before you kissed me. Could we talk about what each of us likes?” Then give your partner a chance to decide whether now is a good time.

Speaking to Men’s Health, sex therapist Laurie Mintz suggests discussing how each person likes to be approached and how they’d like to turn down an invitation. Read Gigi Engle’s reporting on initiating sex.

Leave room for the answer

“I’d like to kiss you. Would you like that?” is a whole question. Wait for the answer. If it’s “I’d rather just sit here,” take it at face value. You can say, “I’d like that too,” if you mean it. If your partner goes quiet, pulls away or seems unsure, stop. Give them space to tell you what they want.

Consent is a free choice about a particular activity. Either person can change their mind, including partway through. An earlier yes tells you what someone wanted then. Ask again when you want to try something different. Planned Parenthood’s guide to consent.

Talk through protection

Choose a moment before sex to discuss protection and testing. If condoms are a condition for you, you can say so plainly. “I want to use condoms. I have some at home.” Testing can be its own conversation. “When were we each last tested, and what did the tests cover?” If you’re unsure, it’s fine to say you’ll check and come back to it.

Using condoms correctly every time lowers the risk of many sexually transmitted infections (STIs). Many STIs have no symptoms. A clinician can help you work out which tests make sense for you and when to get them. The CDC’s guide to STI prevention and testing.

Check in afterward

Later, you might think of something you couldn’t put into words at the time. Name one moment your partner will remember. “I liked how slowly we took things yesterday. How did that feel for you?” A walk together or a message the next day can give a quieter person time to answer. If you hear something surprising, try saying back what you understood before you decide together what to do next time.
