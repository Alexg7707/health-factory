Nutrition guide

What’s on the menu?

A ripe pear turns a bowl of oats into breakfast. A can of beans can fill a pita or top a grain bowl. Here are eight ideas, each with a swap for what you have on hand.

Breakfast Lunch Dinner Snacks

Breakfast

Sweet or savory?

Pear and walnut oatmeal

Simmer rolled oats in milk or water until creamy. Top with sliced pear, chopped walnuts and a pinch of cinnamon. Try apple in place of pear, or pumpkin seeds in place of walnuts.

Tomato and egg toast

Top a slice of whole-grain toast with a sliced hard-boiled egg, tomato and dill. For a plant-based version, spread the toast with hummus and add thin slices of radish.

The World Health Organization recommends eating a variety of vegetables, fruit, whole grains and legumes. Oats, whole-grain bread and chickpeas are three ways to bring that variety to breakfast.

WHO guide to a healthy diet

Lunch

In a bowl or a pita.

Warm buckwheat and bean bowl

Toss cooked buckwheat with rinsed canned beans, roasted carrots and chopped parsley. Dress with olive oil and lemon juice while the grains are still warm. Cooked lentils work here too.

Tuna and dill pita

Stir canned tuna, plain yogurt and chopped dill together. Tuck the filling into a pita with lettuce and grated carrot. For a bean filling, mash white beans with lemon juice and dill.

Packing a tuna pita for later? Keep it chilled in an insulated lunch bag with two frozen ice packs. If there’s a refrigerator where you’re headed, put the pita in it when you arrive.

USDA guide to packing lunch safely

Dinner

Roast a tray or simmer a pot.

Roasted vegetables with chickpeas and bulgur

Cook bulgur according to the package directions. Roast chunks of zucchini and bell pepper until tender, then toss with the grains and cooked chickpeas. Finish with olive oil, lemon and herbs. Brown rice makes a good substitute for bulgur.

Red lentil and tomato soup

Soften a chopped onion and carrot in a little oil. Add rinsed red lentils, chopped tomatoes and enough water to cover. Simmer until the lentils are tender, adding more water as needed. Stir in spinach or a handful of herbs and serve with bread.

Fresh, frozen and canned vegetables can all work in these dishes. The World Health Organization recommends choosing frozen and canned varieties with no added sugars and little added salt. WHO guidance on fruit and vegetables

Snacks

For when you’re hungry.

Yogurt with berries

Spoon plain yogurt into a bowl, add berries and sprinkle with sunflower seeds. Plain soy yogurt works too. Try sliced banana when berries are out of season.

Vegetables and hummus

Cut carrots and bell peppers into strips and serve with hummus and whole-grain crackers. For a quick dip, mash cooked white beans with olive oil and a squeeze of lemon.

For something to drink, try water or unsweetened tea. The CDC suggests keeping a refillable bottle nearby. You may need more water in hot weather or when you’re more active. CDC guide to water and other drinks
