Running

Your first run starts with a walk

Pick a familiar stretch of pavement. Before you start jogging, decide where you’ll walk again. That small decision can change how the first mile feels.

In this story

Plan the first walk break

There’s an awkward moment on a first run when another runner passes and you suddenly feel obliged to keep up. A walk break you chose before leaving home is easier to take. It already has a place in the outing.

That’s the idea behind Olympian Jeff Galloway’s run-walk method. Start mixing walking with running early, while you still feel fresh. He suggests easing between the two paces and adjusting the intervals to suit the runner. Read Galloway’s account in Men’s Health UK, March 4, 2025.

Try a timed session

If you’d like someone else to choose the intervals, the NHS offers a free Couch to 5K plan. Its first session opens with five minutes of walking. Run for one minute and walk for 90 seconds, seven times. After one last minute of running, walk for five minutes to cool down. See the first week of the NHS plan.

Pick a route you could describe in one sentence, such as “twice around the park, past the water fountain.” On unfamiliar streets it’s easy to spend the whole outing checking your phone for turns.

Leave room for the next run

The NHS schedules a rest day between outings. When you get home, jot down one detail worth remembering. Maybe the quiet lane was easier to run along, or the return journey took longer than you guessed. That’s useful information when you lace up again. How NHS Couch to 5K works.

Find company at your pace

Before showing up to a run club, ask the organizer two questions. Does anyone take planned walk breaks? Does someone stay with the last group? The answers tell you more about your first evening than a photo of smiling runners at the finish.
