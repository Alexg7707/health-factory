Hobbies

One cup, three sketches

Set your coffee mug by a window. The handle gives you a curve to follow, and the daylight throws a shape across the table. That’s a whole first drawing session, right there in the kitchen.

The kitchen sketch

Fold a scrap of paper into thirds and put the mug where it casts a shadow. In the first panel, trace its outline with your eyes as you draw. Look through the hole in the handle. Is the opening round, oval or almost a triangle? In the second panel, shade just the patch of darkness on the table. In the third, put the outline and the shadow together. Line the panels up and see which cup looks as though you could pick it up.

A three-photo walk

Take the same exercise outdoors with a phone and one subject. Circles work well on a familiar block. Look for a bicycle wheel, a drain cover and the rim of a planter. Try one picture close enough to fill the frame, one from across the street and one that catches the shape in a reflection. Pick three photographs and arrange them in an order you like. Give the little series a title.

A bird in the margins

Watch one bird from a window or park bench. Write down where it landed, how it moved and what it did just before it flew off. “Hopped twice, tilted its head, pulled something from the grass” will bring the moment back later, even if you never identify the bird. If keeping a tally appeals to you, the events calendar has two winter bird counts with different ways to join.

Picking a second session

Which detail do you still want to look at? If it was the shadow, leave tomorrow’s mug in the same place and draw it at a different hour. If it was the wheel, pick another shape for your next walk. If it was the bird, go back to the same bench. You already have a subject for round two.
